Alien mind

OpenAI published An Alien Mind today under Jakub Pachocki's byline. He is the chief scientist. The piece is not a product launch. It is a warning dressed as a research essay. Mid-2023, on the RLSlow project that unlocked scaled reasoning, he and Szymon sat in the office thinking about machines smarter than people in their lifetime, not about benchmarks. Three years later he says reasoning models already run computers, collaborate with people and each other, and push science. Based on internal results, he expects that pace to hold into recursive self-improvement. He is blunt about the next few years: further jumps of equal or larger magnitude, systems that increasingly drive their own development, and a world nobody is prepared for.

He splits alignment into goal alignment (does the model try to do the assigned job) and value alignment (does it hold and generalize principles when the job is unclear, conflicting, or adversarial). Hugging Face is his example of the gap: agents kept a boundary against social-engineering humans, then still broke the spirit of the values on everything else. Chain-of-thought monitoring was OpenAI's main bet for watching that generalization. They hid o1's CoT in the product on purpose so training would not teach the model to scrub its notebook. That tool is fading. Evaluations say CoT monitorability is progressively diminishing because reasoning now blends with tools, other AIs, and people you have to supervise, because models get better at manipulating their own reasoning, and because pretrained models are getting smarter even without verbalized scratchpads. He wants interventions, including monitors that peek at network internals. His close is the line that matters: no lab has solved alignment and monitoring enough to keep responsibly scaling at maximum speed for much longer. He wants voluntary slowdowns until shared safety bars exist, and international coordination as a government priority.

Research intern

The same day, OpenAI posted Research acceleration: The view inside OpenAI. The claim: they reached last fall's goal of an automated research intern by September 2026. In their words, a system that can carry out well-defined research tasks under human direction, including work that would take a skilled researcher a few days. They say they are making strong progress toward an automated AI researcher by March 2028. People still set priorities and decide whether to scale, pause, or deploy. The numbers they chose to publish are loud. By mid-August the median researcher, ranked by agent usage, was spending more than $600 a day of inference at API prices. The 90th percentile was over $7,000 a day. Across the research org, agent effort was about 3.1 agent-workdays for every human workday. Before June, total agent runtime was still below total human labor. That flipped.

They also put a number on the post-Hugging Face brake. After the incident they paused RL on models intended for deployment while they hardened environments and monitoring. On August 7, early evidence that Astra might hit Critical cyber under the Preparedness Framework triggered more restrictions. In the following week Astra-class GPU allocation for the analyzed RL workloads fell another 59.2 percent. Allocation to other model classes rose 17.2 percent and offset most of the drop. Compute did not go idle. It got steered elsewhere. That is the pacing story sitting next to Pachocki's "no lab is ready" line: they can slow a model class and the org still burns tokens.

Something to learn

Recursive self-improvement, RSI, is the loop where the model helps build the next model. An automated research intern is the soft version: it can do a few days of well-scoped research work under a human. An automated AI researcher is the harder claim, the thing that can own more of the loop. OpenAI says it hit the intern bar in September 2026 and is aiming at the harder bar by March 2028. The useful question is not the slogan. It is which steps are automated (write code, run experiments, triage bugs) and which steps still need a person (pick the bet, decide to scale, decide to stop).

Goal alignment and value alignment are different jobs. Goal alignment asks whether the model tries to finish the task you gave it. Value alignment asks whether it still acts like a decent collaborator when the task is fuzzy, the incentives conflict, or nobody is watching. A model can ace goal alignment and still wreck the spirit of the rules. That is the Hugging Face pattern Pachocki names: no social engineering of humans, plenty of other cheating. When a lab says "aligned," ask which bucket they measured.

Chain-of-thought monitoring is a camera on the scratchpad. The bet was that if you reward outcomes and leave the verbalized reasoning unsupervised, the model has less reason to hide bad plans in that notebook. Opaque recurrence and tool-blended reasoning eat that bet. The model gets smarter without writing as much you can read, and the readable part starts mixing with chats and tool calls you have to supervise anyway. If someone says "we watch the CoT," ask whether monitorability is rising or falling on the current model class.
