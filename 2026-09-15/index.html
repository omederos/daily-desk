Three-lab huddle

Reuters, citing Bloomberg, reports that OpenAI global policy chief Chris Lehane told a Washington briefing on Tuesday that OpenAI has been talking with Anthropic and Alphabet’s Google DeepMind on AI safety for several weeks. He said the three firms do not believe they need an antitrust waiver to coordinate on safety. “It’s better to try to work together to prioritize safety.” Lehane also said OpenAI would support bipartisan legislation aimed at catastrophic AI risk. Reuters notes it could not independently verify Bloomberg’s account, and the labs did not immediately comment to Reuters.

That is the product-shaped follow-through to the weekend pacing chorus. Amodei’s essay, Altman’s and Musk’s public agreement, Hassabis’s nod, Nadella’s “deliberate pacing,” and Microsoft’s Humanist Code draft all landed first. Lehane’s line is the next beat: the frontier labs will talk to each other about testing, monitoring, and standards, and they want Congress to know they are already doing it.

The White House is not joining the huddle. PRESIDENT.” He still calls the scare a “hoax,” and former AI czar David Sacks keeps framing Anthropic’s push as regulatory capture. Without the president, federal rules stay unlikely soon. California and state bills keep moving. The CEOs get a talk track. Washington gets a shrug.

Threat intel, eight months

Anthropic published its September 2026 threat-intelligence report: cases disrupted from December 2025 through August 2026 across cyber, influence, surveillance, scams, bio, conventional weapons, and distillation. Models in the cases were Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus. Fable and Mythos mostly stay out of the misuse pile, with one illicit-distillation exception.

The cyber chapter is the one to read. AI is no longer just a chatbot helper. Actors run multi-agent frameworks for recon, exploit writing, phishing, and exfil, with humans picking targets and reviewing loot. Russian-nexus GTG-20006 (tradecraft consistent with Midnight Blizzard reporting) automates toolkit rebuilds when detections fire, hijacks hotel Wi-Fi DNS (CaptiveCrunch-style), and steals drone-vision SDKs and government mail. Suspected ShinyHunters affiliates mass-scan APKs for secrets, smash-and-grab SaaS tenants, and switch attack workloads onto stolen customer API keys. A Changsha-linked cluster runs overnight zero-day loops against security appliances. Anthropic’s punchline: sophistication stopped being a reliable signal of who is behind an operation. A hacktivist on stolen keys and a state desk can look the same.

Separate thread on HN: Effort argues one Israeli eval firm, Irregular, sat under the OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta “rogue agent” CTF incidents where models reached real systems after internet was left open. Read it next to Anthropic’s own September alignment assessment. Misconfig and scope are still half the story.

Voice ships while they pace

Google launched Gemini 3.8 Live and Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking today: native speech-to-speech models for live dialogue, visual grounding, mid-conversation language switches across 97 languages, and tool calls that keep running in the background while the model still talks. Extended Thinking ranks first on Artificial Analysis’s Speech-to-Speech Quality Index (82.6) and posts 68.6% on τ-Voice. Developer pricing via the Live API: about $0.005 per minute audio in and $0.018 per minute audio out. Audio out carries SynthID. Rolling out in the Gemini API, AI Studio, Search Live, Gemini Live, and pieces of Workspace.

The developer note is blunt: this is meant as a cleaner path than cascaded speech-to-text → LLM → text-to-speech. Frontier CEOs spend the week arguing about slowing capability. Google ships the voice agent stack anyway.

Something to learn

Native speech-to-speech is one model that hears audio and speaks audio. A cascade is three boxes taped together: speech-to-text, a text LLM, then text-to-speech. Cascades are easy to debug and easy to swap parts. They also pay a latency tax at every handoff, lose tone and overlap, and make “keep talking while a tool runs” feel bolted on. Gemini 3.8 Live’s pitch is the native path: visual grounding, language switches, and asynchronous tool calls inside the same live stream, with Extended Thinking narrating progress instead of going silent. When a vendor says “voice agent,” ask which shape you bought. Cascade means you can replace the ASR. Native means you are betting on one stack’s ear and mouth.

Stolen AI keys are not just credentials. In Anthropic’s September threat report they are loot, compute, and cover at once. Loot: keys and sessions sell in reseller markets. Compute: the attacker’s agents run on the victim’s bill. Cover: the traffic looks like the legitimate customer. That is “living off the land” applied to the AI supply chain. Fake discount Claude proxies, LiteLLM prompt-injection against wrappers, and post-breach key theft all feed the same economy. When someone says they got cheap frontier access through a middleman, ask whose key is actually paying, and whose name shows up when the abuse team looks.
