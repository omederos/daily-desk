Gemini 3.8 TTS

Google (Wed): Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS turn text-to-speech from preset voices into a voice design studio. Flash is for character work and creative direction from natural-language prompts. Flash-Lite is the high-volume, cheaper line for dubbing and voice agents. Live in Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, Gemini Notebook, and Google Vids (Flash-Lite in Vids today; Enterprise API soon).

You can invent a voice across 100+ languages and dialects, pick from 2,000+ production voices, or replicate from about 30 seconds of audio with consent checks. Line-by-line stage directions, two-speaker scenes, and scripted backchannels like |mhm| are built in. Provenance is the trust half: SynthID watermarking and C2PA credentials on the audio. Voice replication is blocked in Illinois, Texas, the EEA, the UK, Switzerland, and India. Google claims #1 on Hume’s Voice Design Benchmark (71.4 overall) and top Voice Arena prefs in several languages. This is the voice-agent surface, not another chat model drop.

GPT-6 prompt caching

OpenAI (Tue, still the fresh GPT-6 angle): after Sol and Luna’s list-price cut yesterday, the more useful ship for long agent loops is better prompt caching. Default hit rates are up. Eligible shared prefixes can get up to 90% off cached input (0.1× uncached) inside a 30-minute TTL. New Prompt Caching Dashboard tracks hit rate and cached vs fresh mix. Diagnostics on the Responses API name the miss reasons (tools_changed, model_changed, input_changed).

You can set explicit breakpoints, prewarm a cache, and change reasoning effort mid-conversation without rewriting the cached prefix. Keep tools stable or use allowed_tools / tool_choice: "none". Pricing nuance for GPT-5.6+: cache writes cost 1.25× uncached input; cache reads are 0.1×; minimum cacheable prefix is 1,024 visible tokens. GitHub says related caching work cut fresh prompt processing by more than half across billions of Copilot requests. Sol/Luna cut the sticker. Caching cuts the bill on the second hour of the same agent session.

Astra on a cone course

DrivingBench (HN Wed): Aditya Ramabadran, Simon Mahns, and Tobias Gessler give frontier models a Toyota Corolla’s steering, accelerator, and brakes on a fixed cone course. Progress is share of centerline while staying within 4 meters. Collisions freeze the run. Up to three attempts in one continuous chat, with tools like set_motion and stop_now.

GPT-6 Astra (Codex, medium) DNF’d attempt 1 at 49%, then finished attempt 2 in 5:22 (134.7 m, 24 commands, 6.6M tokens, about $7.74). Claude Fable 5.1 topped out at 45%. Grok 4.6 around 11%. GPT-5.6 Sol stuck near 6%. This is not a self-driving product claim. It is an embodied tool-loop bench with dollar receipts. Only Astra completed the course.

Something to learn

Generative voice design is prompting a new vocal identity, then directing the performance, not picking a stock speaker. Flash TTS lets you describe accent, tone, and character in natural language, stage two speakers in one scene, and insert backchannels. The trust half is provenance: SynthID watermarks the audio and C2PA attaches credentials so a clip can carry where it came from. Replication from a 30-second sample needs consent checks, and some regions block it entirely. When a vendor demos “any voice,” ask whether the output is watermarked, whether credentials survive re-encode, and who can request a clone of your voice.

A telemetry-gated feature flag is a remote kill switch on a local path. Claude Code’s AGENTS.md reader shipped as a Mod behind cloud feature flags. With telemetry off, the flags never arrived, so the markdown file on disk did nothing even though the release notes said it would. The model id never changed. The harness just quietly lacked the feature. Anthropic fixed it in 2.1.281 and called it a rollout artifact. When a tool says it reads a local config file, ask whether that path is hardwired or gated by a remote flag system, and what happens when you opt out of telemetry.
