The labels sued

TechCrunch and The Verge have the weekend filing. Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, and other publishers sued Anthropic plus co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann in the Northern District of California. Late Friday. The complaint calls it a "brazen campaign of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted works" to train Claude.

The Verge's numbers: up to $150,000 per work, plus up to $25,000 each time identifiable copyright data was stripped. If a court maxes that out, several billion. Songs named in the coverage include "Ain't No Mountain High Enough," "Livin' On a Prayer," "September," "Hallelujah," and Taylor Swift's "Paper Rings." The suit says Mann used BitTorrent for more than five million pirated books, that employees pulled at least two million more from Pirate Library Mirror, and that Anthropic scraped lyrics from Musixmatch and LyricFind, sites that pay the labels.

This is not the Concord/UMG case from January, and it is not Friday's Pentagon ruling. Same lawyers as Concord and as Bartz, the authors case that ended in a $1.5 billion piracy settlement. Bartz already drew the line: training on copyrighted text can be fair use. Acquiring the pile through piracy is not. The labels are running that theory on lyrics and sheet music, and they named the founders as people, not just the company. Anthropic told TechCrunch it disagrees and will defend itself in court.

Someone else is using your Claude

BleepingComputer this morning: Anthropic is emailing affected users. A bad actor is picking Claude login sessions out of common infostealer dumps, signing in as you, and burning the usage. Sometimes they trigger charges. Anthropic is kicking those sessions, pulling saved cards, and refunding what it can identify. The tell it gave users: limits that refill, then drain, while you are idle.

This is not a Claude hole. Anthropic says the malware is unrelated to the product. Vidar, LummaC2, StealC, RedLine, and Acreed on Windows. Atomic Stealer, also called AMOS, on a small number of Macs. The usual path is a junk download. One person who posted the email had grabbed a pirated game. The important part is how the login works. Infostealers copy an already-authenticated browser cookie. Password and 2FA never get asked again. Signing out of Claude kills the stolen session. It does not clean the machine. If the malware is still there, the next login is another cookie in the dump.

Tencent put Hy4 on the Hub

Tencent Hunyuan open-sourced Hy4 preview Friday. The story landed on Hacker News Saturday and is still on the front page this afternoon. The Hub card and TechNode match on the size: 770 billion parameters total, 49 billion awake per token, context over a million tokens. Apache 2.0. Hugging Face's file list puts the safetensors pile nearer 780 billion. Same family either way. Weights also on ModelScope. An FP8 pack is there if you do not want the fat one.

Tencent's own blind test, 163 internal experts, 203 engineering tasks: Hy4 preview 2.99 out of 4, GLM 5.3 at 2.92, Kimi K3 at 2.94. Treat that as their homework. The serving recipe is the useful number. Official vLLM and SGLang images run the FP8 build at tensor-parallel-size 8. Eight GPUs, not a desk card. API list price on TokenHub is $0.834 per million input tokens, $2.501 per million output, $0.042 on cache hits. WorkBuddy and CodeBuddy are free for two weeks. Hacker News spent as much time on the cache math as on the pelican SVG traces. Cache hits are where long coding sessions actually bill.

THE DECODER covers Google Research's WikiSkill paper, posted Saturday. The model does not keep learning in the weights. After each run it writes a wiki of what failed and what worked, then packages that into gated "Agent Skills." Skills can roll back. The wiki does not. Gemini-3.5-Flash went from 49.5 percent to 68.1 percent average across their five benches. Qwen-3.6-27B from 39.4 to 63.3. Biggest jumps on math and spreadsheets. Long-document tasks barely moved. This is a notes file with a gate, not a brain that updates itself.

BleepingComputer also caught Anthropic's Claude Code limit math on Saturday. Permanent +25 percent on standard weekly limits for Pro, Max, Team, and seat Enterprise, starting September 14. The temporary +50 percent stays through September 13. Compared with today that is a 17 percent cut. Anthropic deleted the first thread and said so itself. If the old baseline was 100, you are at 150 now and land at 125 in two weeks.

GitHub issue #66504 hit the HN front page today, 118 points and a pile of comments for a June ticket. Claude Code appends a session URL to commit messages and PR descriptions by default on web and Remote Control sessions. Boris Cherny closed it August 17 because attribution.sessionUrl: false exists. Commenters say recent builds mark every session remote, so the link is back in public git history. The setting is opt-out, not opt-in, and it is easy to miss until the URL is already pushed.

Something to learn

A session cookie is already a key. After you log in, the browser stores a blob that says "this person is in." Infostealers copy that blob. The attacker never types your password and never sees your 2FA prompt, because the cookie is the proof. That is why Anthropic can sign you out of Claude and still tell you to clean the machine. A new login on an infected PC just mints a fresh cookie for the same dump. Rotate passwords after the malware is gone, not before.

Fair use is about what you do with a work. Piracy is about how you got the file. Bartz already split those. A judge said training Claude on copyrighted books could be fair use, then hit Anthropic for $1.5 billion because the books were torrented. The music publishers are running the same split on lyrics and sheet music, and they named the founders. If you are building a model, the license on the dataset is not a vibe. It is the acquisition story a court will read first.

Prompt cache is why a "cheap" API still prints a fat bill. Providers keep the start of your conversation, the system prompt, the tools, the files, in GPU memory. The next turn only pays full price for the new tokens, and a much smaller cache-hit price for everything you already sent. Hy4's list is $0.834 per million input and $0.042 on a cache hit, about 5 percent. Stay on one provider for the whole session or the cache misses and you pay the fat rate again. That is the real "runs on X GPU" for APIs. The card is whoever still has your prefix in memory.
