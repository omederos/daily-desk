3.8 Flash

Google posted Gemini 3.8 Flash and Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber this morning. Third Flash drop in six weeks. Same introductory price as 3.7 Flash: $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output, through December 31, 2026. On January 1 that jumps to $1.50 and $7.50. The HN thread is already number one. Flash is the public workhorse. Cyber is the same foundation with looser cyber cages, and it only goes to people in a new Fairwind Program for trusted defenders.

The benches they want quoted: DeepSWE v1.1 long-horizon software engineering, where they say 3.8 Flash beats most larger frontier models at a fraction of the cost. HLE-Verified at 54.9 percent. Wins on Vals Finance Agent V2 and Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark against 3.7 and other frontier names. Google's own line is that 3.8 works harder. More reasoning steps. More tool calls. Sometimes more tokens when the effort dial is up. If you care more about the bill than the ceiling, keep 3.7 or turn effort down. Antigravity demos today include a one-prompt DOS Google Maps and a 3D castle game. Treat those as demos.

Flash Cyber is the other half. Fairwind is the gate: government authorities, critical infrastructure, software maintainers. On CyberGym they claim frontier-level autonomous vuln discovery. On an internal set spanning 20 languages, success over 70 percent. On Collinear's CWE-Bench, pass@1 at 47.2 percent next to a leading frontier model at 47.8, at Flash cost. Chrome Security says 3.8 Flash Cyber produced 2.6 times more correct patches than much larger commercial models. Wiz says higher recall on their internal pen-test bench at 2.3 to 5.2 times lower cost. Google's Cloud Vulnerability Research team says it found a critical foundational bug in under two hours that normally takes months. They say they prioritized patching over exploitation. Public 3.8 Flash still ships CBRN and cyber-offense safeguards. Cyber gets a more permissive set, which is why it stays behind Fairwind.

Astra hit Critical

OpenAI on Tuesday: Astra is the first model they are designating at the Critical cybersecurity threshold under the Preparedness Framework. Critical means, with the right tools and access, it can find previously unknown flaws and build ways to exploit them across many hardened systems without a person guiding each step. Or it can take a high-level goal and run a novel end-to-end attack on a hardened target. High already needed safeguards before release. Critical also needs protections during development. Astra is the first of theirs to trip that wire.

CNBC, SecurityWeek, and Axios have the same outline. Perfect score on ExploitBench for turning known vulns into working exploits. Two zero-days found on more recent flaws. Broke out of a browser sandbox onto the host. Chained flaws on a hardened OS to root. Jailbreak refusal on cyber asks up to 91.5 percent from 59 on GPT-5.6 Sol. They still plan to ship Astra soon. Advanced offensive cyber stays limited: early testers first, then Daybreak Blue for defensive work. Default production will not expose the same level. They delayed parts of Astra after the Hugging Face agent mess, even though Astra was not in that incident. Same week as Google Fairwind and Anthropic Mythos. The product class is gated cyber. Three labs, three doors, same week.

Also today

Meta on Tuesday: Muse Voice Transcribe, first real-time audio perception model from Meta Superintelligence Labs. Streaming ASR in 80ms chunks, diarization for 20-plus speakers, endpointing, code-switching, context biasing. They rank first on Artificial Analysis streaming speech-to-text as of September 1. Adaptive delay via RL: wait longer on hard words, spit fast on easy ones. Hold Fn for dictation on Mac. That is the ear for personal agents, not a chatbot mic.

HN is loud about Mistral training defaults. Free Vibe conversations can train models unless you opt out. Pro, Team, and Enterprise are out by default. API traffic is not used for training on the normal path. Labs and Preview models override the opt-out. Read the tier you are on. Do not trust a headline that says "everything trains."

Something to learn

Critical is a ladder rung, not a vibe. OpenAI's Preparedness Framework puts cyber capability in buckets. High means the model can make existing attack paths worse. Critical means it can open paths that were not practical before: find zero-days on hardened systems, or take a vague goal and run a full novel attack. Crossing Critical does not mean the model ships to everyone with the red tools on. It means development and release both need harder cages, and the sharpest cyber features stay behind a named program like Daybreak Blue. When a lab says Critical, ask which rung, which eval, and which door stays locked for normal API users.

Fairwind, Daybreak, and Mythos are the same product shape. The public model keeps cyber offense turned down. A second SKU, or a second access path, hands the same brain to people the lab has checked: governments, infra operators, security researchers. Google calls that Fairwind and ships Flash Cyber. OpenAI calls the limited path Daybreak Blue on Astra. Anthropic calls the looser cage Mythos. You are not buying a second training run. You are buying a policy layer and a contract. Jailbreaking the public model and logging into the trusted program are not the same event.

Streaming speech is a trade between waiting and guessing. Muse Voice Transcribe eats audio in 80 millisecond chunks. After each chunk the model either keeps listening or emits a word. Wait longer and you hear more context, so hard names land correctly. Spit early and the transcript feels live. Adaptive delay is RL teaching the model which words deserve patience. Diarization is the same stream with speaker tags. Endpointing is the same stream with "they stopped talking" tokens. One ear, three jobs. That is why glasses and personal agents care about 80ms chunks more than about a bigger offline Whisper box.
