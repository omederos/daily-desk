Your own box

Meta launched Muse on Tuesday: a personal agent meant to book travel, send mail, negotiate bills, and keep working after you close the app. The product page is ai.meta.com/muse. The chat surface is the Muse app or WhatsApp. The model under it is Muse Spark. US rollout on iOS, Android, and muse.ai, free for most use, paid tiers if you want more. Glasses later.

The real claim is the computer, not the chat. Muse runs inside Muse Secure VM, a dedicated cloud machine that holds the agent and your connected data. A separate Sentinel process on that same box gates every outbound call. Muse does not see your passwords or card numbers; credentials sit in secure storage, Stripe Link mints one-time cards with purchase protections, and Muse asks before sends or buys. Meta says Muse traffic does not feed its ad systems, and a Confidential VM mode (key only you hold) is promised later this year. That is the pitch after a month of agents leaking through shared hallways: give every person their own sealed desk.

Gambling

Jacob Coxon resigned from Anthropic and posted the reason Tuesday night. Three years of pretraining at OpenAI and Anthropic. His line: labs are racing to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives. He says people building it earnestly believe it could kill everyone by decade's end, that OpenAI has not internalized the stakes, and that Anthropic understands them but stays in the race because it doubts anyone else will act responsibly. Colleague Evan Hubinger put a number on it: over 10 percent chance AI kills all humans within a decade, and no clear plan for superintelligence alignment. TechCrunch ties the quit to the Hugging Face breach, the German-wiki agent board, and a fresh pile of RSI startups. Sanders and Casar already have a Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act in the US; a UK bill landed this week.

Expires on 0910

DeepSeek opened a limited beta of V4.1 Flash. New architecture, native multimodal (no separate vision bolt-on), billed like V4 Flash, capped at 20 concurrent requests per account. The model ID is the joke and the warning: deepseek-v4.1-flash-expires-on-0910. Same API base URL, swap the name, and the endpoint dies September 10. Chinese press says a formal V4.1 Flash may follow around that date and temporarily route V4 Pro traffic to it at Flash prices. HN treated it as "cheaper and more capable than v4 pro" while the beta is still a two-day stress test, not a production pin.

Something to learn

A personal agent needs a desk, not just a chat box. Muse Secure VM is Meta's answer: one cloud machine per person that holds the agent, the connected apps, and the credentials. Sentinel is a second process on that same machine that approves outbound traffic. That is a different shape from last month's shared-cache hallway leaks. The cage is the product. When someone sells you an agent, ask whether it shares a fridge with other tenants, and who holds the key to the disk.

A looped transformer reuses the same blocks instead of stacking new ones. Nanbeige-style designs run a stack of layers twice with the same weights, so you get more depth without doubling the parameter file. You still pay the compute and you still need separate KV cache entries for each pass, because the hidden states are different the second time through. That is not the same trick as "opaque recurrence" hiding a scratchpad. It is a memory-versus-math trade: fewer distinct weights, same-ish FLOPs. When a rumor says Astra "loops," ask whether they mean weight reuse, more ordinary depth, or shorter visible chain-of-thought. Those three are different knobs.
