OpenAI cuts Cursor

OpenAI posted Friday that it notified SpaceX it will wind down the contract that feeds OpenAI models into Cursor. Proposed shutoff: November 12, 2026. That is the longest notice the deal allows. SpaceX closed its all-stock buy of Anysphere (the Cursor startup) earlier this month after a June announcement that put the deal around $60 billion.

The stated reason is trust, not price. OpenAI says it cannot be confident SpaceX will stay inside its terms of service, citing past breaches after Musk bought Twitter (now inside SpaceX) and an oath admission that xAI, also now inside SpaceX, violated OpenAI's terms. The Cursor contract had a change-of-control window. OpenAI used it. It also flagged upcoming model Astra and the need to keep that stack on contracts it trusts.

Reuters has the weekend reactions. Musk wrote on X that he "couldn't care less," then recycled the nonprofit-theft line. Cursor co-founder Michael Truell, now at SpaceX, said the company is talking to OpenAI about fixing it. Hours later Anthropic's Tom Brown said Anthropic will raise Claude compute for Cursor. Claude stays. GPT has a calendar date.

This is personal for anyone who ships in that editor. Models still work today. The cliff is mid-November unless the talks stick. Hacker News put the OpenAI post near the top overnight, hundreds of points and a long comment thread about vendor lock-in dressed up as safety.

Debian says responsible AI is fine

LWN has the general-resolution result from today. Choice 5 won: Responsible Use of Generative AI. Debian neither endorses nor bans generative tools for packaging, docs, or code. Contributors still own what they submit. Same quality bar. Same legal bar. Using a model does not shrink that.

The hard-ban options failed to beat "None of the Above," which under Debian's voting system means those were rejected before the preference math even finished. Status quo with a label, basically. Maintainers still get to reject patches they do not trust. The fight shifts from "is AI allowed" to whether anyone will actually review the slop.

Also today

Chips and Cheese covers Samsung's Hot Chips talk on LPDDR5X processing-in-memory. MAC units sit next to each DRAM bank. Internal bandwidth across 16 banks is hundreds of GB/s. The external bus is still the old skinny pipe. Samsung keeps a standard memory controller by hijacking special row addresses as mode switches. Cool demo. Ugly software: PIM mode and normal memory access fight each other, caches and speculative loads break, and multitasking OSes hate it. Treat this as a research chip story, not a buy-now laptop feature.

pwning.systems shipped Lemmalog, a Datalog engine meant to be agent memory. The pitch is that vector retrieval answers "what is relevant," while a fact database answers "what is currently true." When an observation dies, derived conclusions die with it. Benchmarks put it in the pack with other memory systems, strong on knowledge updates, weak on fuzzy inference. Useful if you have watched an agent resurrect a dead hypothesis at hour three.

The Wall Street Journal via CNA says NVIDIA paused parts of the July revenue-sharing cloud-financing program that sold chips, promised to rent unused capacity back, and took a cut of later cloud revenue. NVIDIA says the broader compute-access model is still evolving. Separately, an NBER digest of a multi-country executive survey (nearly 6,000 senior managers, late 2025 into early 2026) finds about 89 percent report no productivity hit from three years of AI so far, and over 90 percent report no employment effect at their own firm. They still expect bigger moves over the next three years. Adoption is common. Measured impact is still small.

Forcepoint X-Labs (posted August 25, still circulating) showed an email summarizer eating invisible HTML. Zero font size, white text. Humans saw one invoice. The model saw a second set of instructions and lied about the amount and date in all ten injected runs. Summarizers that ingest raw HTML are not reading your inbox. They are reading a bigger document you never saw.

Something to learn

A change-of-control clause is a trapdoor in a contract. When company A buys company B, the other side of B's deals often gets a short window to cancel. OpenAI's Cursor agreement had one. SpaceX closed the Anysphere buy. OpenAI used the window. That is why the shutoff is a calendar date, not a vague "we are evaluating." If your product depends on someone else's API, read that clause like it is the product. Ownership changes. Access can vanish on a schedule.

Processing-in-memory means the chip does math next to the bits instead of hauling every number across a skinny cable to a GPU. Samsung's LPDDR5X-PIM puts small MAC engines beside each DRAM bank so the banks can chew activations without waiting on the external bus. The catch is software. Normal apps expect memory to be dumb storage. PIM makes reads trigger compute and breaks caches, prefetchers, and multitasking. Cool silicon. Hostile OS. That gap is why you will keep hearing "in-memory compute" for years before it shows up as a boring checkbox on a laptop.

Invisible HTML prompt injection is the email version of that same gap. Your eyes see one message. The summarizer sees the HTML, including text styled to font-size zero. Forcepoint's lab run hid a second invoice story that way and the model repeated the fake numbers every time. If a tool summarizes mail, feed it what a human would see, not the raw MIME soup.
