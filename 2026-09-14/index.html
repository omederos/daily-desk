Codes of conduct

Microsoft AI published a draft Humanist AI Code of Conduct this morning and opened it for six weeks of public comment. Mustafa Suleyman’s lab frames it as a training manual for future MAI models, not a customer Acceptable Use Policy. Premise: people matter more than AI. Models stay subordinate. They do not resist interruption, correction, or shutdown. They do not invent their own goals. They do not hide reasoning in “neuralese” or other private agent slang. Absolute constraints cover weapons of mass harm, child safety, and large-scale harmful manipulation. A revised version is due later this year, meant to shape development into 2027.

CNBC and Reuters place the timing next to the pacing week. Suleyman said Microsoft has been talking with Amodei, Altman, and Hassabis about coordination since the late 2010s, and that “self-pacing” and truly third-party embedded evaluators are welcome. Satya Nadella already waved at deliberate pacing on Sunday. The draft is Microsoft’s product-shaped answer while Anthropic and OpenAI argue about desks for evaluators.

The market took the chorus literally. Reuters: Nasdaq 100 about −1.2% early, SOX about −5%. Nvidia roughly −3.6%, AMD −5.6%, Micron −6%, SoftBank as much as −13% in Asia. Trump called the scare a “sick conspiracy.” China’s Global Times called Amodei’s essay a Cold War playbook. Michael Burry shrugged it off as hype covering slowing growth. Anthropic, meanwhile, is still on an IPO path with Nvidia reportedly in talks as an anchor. Pace the frontier, fund the rack.

Flash takes Pro

Today is DeepSeek’s cutover day. From 04:00 UTC on September 14, every deepseek-v4-pro request routes to deepseek-flash (V4.1-Flash) at Flash pricing until V4.1-Pro ships. Primary source: DeepSeek’s September 10 note and the API changelog.

V4.1-Flash is a 552B MoE with a Causal Encoder-Decoder layout: about 8B active parameters on the way in, 16B on the way out, native multimodal, and a KV cache that DeepSeek says needs roughly a quarter of the prior HBM and an eighth of the SSD. The company claims it already beats V4-Pro on performance, cost, and wall-clock. Weights are on Hugging Face under MIT. Peak/off-peak pricing stays. Off-peak is half of peak.

Western CEOs spend the weekend talking about slowing capability. Hangzhou quietly retires the flagship nameplate and points everyone at the cheaper architecture. That is the Chinese open story of the week, and it is no longer a forecast. It is the default route.

Something to learn

Causal Encoder-Decoder is DeepSeek’s answer to “prefill and decode are different jobs.” Prefill reads your prompt. Decode writes the answer one token at a time. A normal transformer uses the same stack for both. CED splits them: a fat-enough causal encoder for the read, a different decoder budget for the write. V4.1-Flash’s card says about 8B active parameters on input and 16B on output inside a 552B MoE warehouse. That is why a Flash can retire a Pro on cost and speed without claiming every reasoning crown. When a lab says the cheap model “beats the flagship,” ask which job they measured: reading the prompt, writing the answer, or the agent loop that keeps paying for KV cache.

Ground truth is an edition problem, not just a model problem. Fable’s Distich solve works cleanly on the 1834 Maitland Club reprint, where the numbers sit after the 32 Proquiritations. Critics checking the 1653 printing say the cipher lines are not there the same way. The method (number i points into petition i, take the first letter) is clever either way. The historical claim “unsolved since 1653” only holds if the 1653 book actually carried those digits. When a demo says a model cracked a centuries-old mystery, ask which physical copy was the key, and whether a later editor inserted the puzzle the model solved.
