<h2 id="near-miss">Near miss</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/18/politics/us-military-ai-false-intelligence-china-ship">CNN</a> (Fri, exclusive): A US special operations analyst used a chatbot on intelligence reporting about a Chinese ship’s manifest, then used AI again to package the findings into a standard intel report. The report said the ship carried something it did not. Armed personnel were preparing to board. Planes were in the air. Officials caught the error just before the operation. One source told CNN the report was “entirely false” and “almost started a war.”</p>
<p>The bot fused open-source material with secret signals intelligence and spat out a trusted-looking product. It was not clear whether the chatbot was commercial or a government wrapper. A former senior official’s line: internal tools are “mostly just copies of the commercial stuff wearing lipstick.” Pentagon and US Special Operations Command Pacific did not comment. Hegseth’s January AI Acceleration Strategy is still the push. The missing piece is a shared standard for verifying AI-generated targeting intel before humans move.</p>
<p>Read it next to this week’s extinction essays and palace asks. The near-term risk is not a model that wakes up. It is a human who trusts a polished false report under time pressure.</p>

<h2 id="kill-switch">Kill switch</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/18/governor-newsom-issues-executive-order-to-accelerate-independent-oversight-and-advance-the-creation-of-an-ai-kill-switch/">Governor Newsom</a> (Fri): California executive order accelerates independent oversight of frontier AI and advances work on an emergency shutoff, or “kill switch,” for frontier models. A convened expert group has about two months to recommend how to harden the state’s AI safety laws. Ideas on the table: third parties writing safety plans for frontier labs, and requiring a verified emergency shutoff. Newsom frames it against a quiet federal government and CEOs who asked for pacing.</p>
<p>This is not a switch thrown today. It is a state forcing the paperwork for one. Same week Amodei, Altman, and company argued for slower frontier runs, California is writing the enforcement shape into an order.</p>

<h2 id="forum">Forum to monorepo</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.hacktron.ai/blog/hacking-openai">Hacktron</a> (write-up public Fri; chain July 25) and <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/18/openai-hacked-anthropic-claude-chatbot">The Guardian</a>: Harsh Jaiswal, Mohan Pedhapati, and Rahul Maini chained a heap overflow in <span class="mono">libheif</span> (via ImageMagick on OpenAI’s Discourse forum at community.openai.com) with an OpenAI SSO identity flaw. Result: takeover of employee ChatGPT and Codex accounts, then access into connected GitHub. Proof was a harmless PR in the internal <span class="mono">openai/openai</span> monorepo. Full timeline under 72 hours. OpenAI patched in about 14 hours and paid a $6,500 bounty for the OpenAI-side finding. Guardian notes the team used Claude to help generate exploit code.</p>
<p>The lesson is the connector graph. ChatGPT and Codex were logged into GitHub, Slack, mail. Account takeover was not “just chat.” It was a keyring. HN put the write-up near the top of the board this afternoon.</p>

<h2 id="learn">Something to learn</h2>
<p>An AI kill switch is an emergency shutoff for a frontier model or the systems that serve it. Not a metaphor. A real control plane that can stop training, serving, or both when something goes wrong. Newsom’s order does not install one tonight. It asks experts how California could require labs to build one and prove it works under independent check. The hard questions are boring and load-bearing: who holds the button, what exactly turns off (weights, endpoints, fine-tunes, customer agents), how you test it without waiting for a crisis, and whether a verified switch is different from a blog post saying “we can pause.” When a politician says “kill switch,” ask for the runbook, the owner, and the audit, not the slogan.</p>
<p>A diffusion language model does not write left to right one token at a time. It starts from noise and denoises many tokens in parallel, the way image diffusion cleans a picture. Autoregressive models (the GPT/Claude shape) are stuck generating token N before token N+1, which keeps GPUs memory-bound. Stefano Ermon’s Inception bet is that parallel denoise is the inference-time version of the RNN-to-transformer jump: same quality class as flash/mini models, much better mapping to GPU math, and a serving stack you have to invent because vLLM was not built for it. When someone says their model is “faster,” ask whether they bought custom silicon for an autoregressive decode, or changed the generation algorithm so ordinary GPUs stay busy.</p>
