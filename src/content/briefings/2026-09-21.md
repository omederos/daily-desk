<h2 id="grok">Grok 4.7</h2>
<p><a href="https://x.ai/news/grok-4-7">SpaceXAI</a> (Mon) and <a href="https://github.blog/changelog/2026-09-21-grok-4-7-is-now-available-in-github-copilot/">GitHub Copilot changelog</a> (Mon): Grok 4.7 is live. Same list price as 4.6 ($2 / $6 per million input / output tokens), larger base, longer RL on multi-hour tasks, and a louder pitch on self-verification and jailbreak resistance. CursorBench 4.0 and Terminal-Bench are the scoreboard they want you to look at. Available in Cursor, Grok Build, the API, Vercel AI Gateway (40% off into Sep 27), and a gradual Copilot model-picker rollout for paid plans. Fast variant: twice the output speed at twice the price.</p>
<p>After a public delay talk about models that quit early and skim their own work, the ship note is basically: we trained it to stay on the problem and check the answer. Pricing stays flat while the card claims frontier price-performance on long coding jobs. That is the Monday product beat next to the pacing politics still swirling from the weekend suit.</p>

<h2 id="muse">Amazon locks Muse out</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/998078/amazon-blocks-meta-muse-ai-agent-shopping">The Verge</a> / <a href="https://www.businessinsider.com/amazon-blocks-meta-muse-ai-agent-shopping-site-2026-9">Business Insider</a> (Mon, GeekWire break Sun): Meta’s Muse personal agent can no longer shop Amazon on a user’s behalf. Muse users hit a popup: continued access by an “unauthorized AI agent” violates Amazon’s Conditions of Use. Amazon says Meta never asked, Muse does not identify itself while browsing, and the agent appears to capture or store customer credentials. Meta’s launch line was that Muse cannot see passwords or payment methods because those live in secure storage. Amazon has asked Meta to remove the marketplace from the Muse experience, and pointed back to the same fight it already had with Perplexity’s Comet.</p>
<p>Agentic shopping stops being a demo the moment the merchant says no. The useful split is not “AI bad.” It is who authorized the session, whether the agent announces itself, and whether the store’s personalization and abuse systems get to see a robot or a fake human.</p>

<h2 id="smart">SMART soft start</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/infrastructure/4735624/faa-ai-air-traffic-controller-system-dc/">Washington Examiner</a> / <a href="https://www.fox5dc.com/news/faa-set-test-ai-air-traffic-system-dc-area-airports-reports-say">FOX 5 DC</a> (Mon), following <a href="https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/faa-tees-up-875m-ai-tool-to-help-manage-air-traffic-congestion/">Ars Technica</a> (Fri): FAA SMART (Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes, and Trajectories) begins its soft launch around Washington: Dulles, Reagan National, BWI, with planning recommendations flowing through the Command Center in Warrenton, Virginia. Ninety-day pilot talk. Part of Air Space Intelligence’s $875M, 12-year FMDS build. The system cross-reads schedules, weather, airport capacity, and airspace constraints to flag congestion before departure. Controllers keep aircraft separation. SMART is a shared planning picture, not autopilot for jets.</p>
<p>Yesterday’s “watch Monday” note lands as a start, not a national flip. Headline writers will say “AI air traffic controller.” The FAA fact sheet says decision support into existing flow management. Believe the runbook over the metaphor.</p>

<h2 id="learn">Something to learn</h2>
<p>An unauthorized shopping agent is a session the merchant did not agree to host. Muse can hold your credentials in a vault and still look like a silent browser that skips Amazon’s agent rules, personalization, and abuse sensors. Amazon’s block is basically three checks: did we authorize this app, does the agent identify itself, and are credentials being captured outside our stack. That is different from “AI should never buy things.” It is closer to OAuth and bot disclosure for checkout. When a personal agent promises to shop for you, ask which stores signed up, how the agent announces itself in logs, and what happens when the merchant says no.</p>
<p>Planning-layer AI is not a control loop. SMART reads schedules, weather, and capacity, then suggests routes and push times into the same flow-management picture airlines and the FAA already share. Controllers still separate planes. Autopilot and collision-avoidance systems are different machines with different certification. Headlines that say “AI air traffic controller” collapse those layers. When a regulator ships an AI tool into a safety-critical domain, ask whether it recommends, or whether it moves metal. Soft launch, human in the loop, and a 90-day pilot are the tells that you are still in the recommend bucket.</p>
