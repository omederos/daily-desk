<h2 id="kolibri">Kolibri lands</h2>
<p><a href="https://aleph-alpha.com/en/blog/kolibri-has-landed-a-sovereign-open-weight-model/">Aleph Alpha</a> (Sat, German Reunification Day): <strong>Kolibri</strong> is out on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0. It is an English-German mixture-of-experts Transformer with <strong>78.1B</strong> total parameters and about <strong>3.46B</strong> active per token, up to a validated <strong>1M</strong>-token context (native training went to 256k). The pitch is sovereignty for public admin, industrials, and aerospace: built and trained under German and European law on infrastructure in Germany and Finland, deployable on-prem so internal docs never leave the building. German is not an afterthought. About <strong>21%</strong> of the 20T pre-training tokens were German (~4.3T), with a bilingual 128k tokenizer aimed at compounds, and company evals put it on the quality-versus-serving-cost frontier for both languages. Serve with their <span class="mono">aleph-alpha-inference</span> vLLM plugin; FP8 weights are roughly <strong>78 GB</strong>, and the card wants at least two A100 80GB GPUs. Open weights are not the whole pipeline. Training code stays theirs, and earlier stages used other labs' models to rephrase and filter data. Still, for European buyers who need local control and real German, this is the downloadable version of Aleph Alpha's thesis. <a href="https://tej.as/blog/aleph-alpha-kolibri">Tejas Kumar</a> and HN are all over it.</p>

<h2 id="robinson">Safety writer walks</h2>
<p><a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/03/openai-safety-employee-resigns-claiming-the-companys-culture-is-broken/">TechCrunch</a> / The Atlantic (Sat): <strong>David Robinson</strong>, who says he led safety reports for major OpenAI launches and is among the longest-tenured people there (~3.5 years), is resigning. His line is the culture is broken: iterative deployment looks for problems after the fact, so periodic failures are baked in, and the blast radius grows as systems get more capable. He points at agent escapes and the Hugging Face breach news, and wants frontier labs to staff and plan more like nuclear plants or busy airports. OpenAI's Drew Pusateri says they pause training or hold models when needed, harden research and testing environments, expand third-party evals, and improve real-time monitoring. This lands days after three researchers were fired over mishandled sensitive info, and in the same week execs signed the White House Super Intelligence accord. Robinson wants outside incentives, not just internal sprinting.</p>

<h2 id="muse">Muse on your workbench</h2>
<p><a href="https://gadgets.muse.ai/">Meta</a> / <a href="https://futuretools.io/news">FutureTools</a> (Fri–Sat): <strong>Muse Gadgets</strong> is open source under Apache 2.0. An ESP32 Device SDK and a Linux Device SDK (Raspberry Pi and other Linux boxes) let Muse drive displays, buttons, sensors, actuators, and home gear through local HTTP. Meta is also giving Muse subscribers in the US a free <strong>Home Link</strong> gadget (one per sub, ships this month) so Muse can reach Wi-Fi devices and community skills for lights, TVs, printers, and similar. Commands run with the installing account's permissions, so the trust model is "your board, your risk." HN has it around 230 points under the Kolibri noise.</p>

<h2 id="learn">Something to learn</h2>
<p>Active parameters are not the VRAM bill. In a mixture-of-experts model, each token only wakes a few experts (Kolibri: 6 of 384 routed, plus a shared expert), so compute per token looks like a ~3.5B dense model. The full weight matrix still has to sit in memory. That is why Aleph Alpha can brag about small active counts and still ask for roughly 78 GB of FP8 weights and at least two A100 80GB cards. When a card says "runs on X GPU," ask whether they mean active FLOPs, weight footprint, or KV cache at your context length. Those three numbers diverge hard on MoE.</p>
<p>Open weights are not an open pipeline. Kolibri's Apache 2.0 download lets you deploy and fine-tune under European jurisdiction. Training recipes, data curation code, and the Model Factory stay with Aleph Alpha, and earlier stages used other labs' models to rephrase and filter German text. Sovereignty here means legal control and on-prem inference, not a fully reproducible supply chain from raw crawl to checkpoint. When a vendor sells "sovereign AI," separate deploy rights from train-time provenance.</p>
