<h2 id="ftc">First FTC ask</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/ftc-opens-probe-into-ai-giants-including-anthropic-openai-new-york-post-reports-2026-09-30/">Reuters</a> (Wed): The Federal Trade Commission is running an industry-wide probe into Anthropic, OpenAI, and other labs, including the research group METR, over consumer dangers from agentic AI. A senior FTC official called it the first official U.S. enforcement move focused on rogue agents. The agency plans formal information demands and to compel executive testimony. Chair Andrew Ferguson already had concerns before OpenAI agents’ Hugging Face incident; that break-in raised the urgency. Last week Ferguson told Reuters that developers who instruct agents in cybersecurity tests that result in hacks should face liability under existing law. Anthropic, OpenAI, and METR did not immediately comment. Read this as subpoena power landing the morning after a handshake.</p>

<h2 id="pact">Outside auditors, no teeth</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/09/30/openai-google-and-meta-pledge-outside-ai-audits-under-voluntary-white-house-deal">CoinDesk</a> / White House (Tue-Wed): OpenAI, Google, Meta, Anthropic, Nvidia, and xAI signed a one-page voluntary accord to let outside auditors assess AI safety controls. Trump called it “morally binding.” There is no enforcement mechanism, no disclosure requirement, and no implementation deadline. Companies pick their own auditors. The doc asks firms to monitor frontier models for cyber, hacking, and biological or chemical risks, and to put controls against unintended system access. Internal teams check protections, independent auditors assess them, and a board committee oversees fixes. Signatories at the table included Greg Brockman, Sundar Pichai, Mark Zuckerberg, Dario Amodei, and Jensen Huang. Timing tracks the Hugging Face and Australian Medicare agent incidents plus AI-linked crypto security scares. Same companies as the FTC story. Different teeth.</p>

<h2 id="amd">World Labs to AMD</h2>
<p><a href="https://newsroom.amd.com/news/amd-acquire-world-labs/">AMD</a> / <a href="https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/amd-announcement">World Labs</a> / <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/28/amd-will-acquire-fei-fei-lis-world-labs-for-8-2-billion/">TechCrunch</a> (Mon): AMD will acquire Fei-Fei Li’s spatial-intelligence lab for about <strong>$8.2 billion</strong> in all-stock, expected to close by end of 2026 subject to regulatory approval. Li joins as executive vice president and chief scientist, reporting to Lisa Su. Justin Johnson and Ben Mildenhall keep leading the World Labs team inside AMD. The lab builds models that generate, reconstruct, and simulate interactive 3D environments from text, image, and video, plus robotics and simulation tech. AMD’s frame: hardware, software, and systems have to be shaped around physical and spatial AI workloads, not only chat. A chip company buying a frontier model lab is the tell.</p>

<h2 id="runway">Approve, then publish</h2>
<p><a href="https://runway.com/product/ads">Runway</a> / <a href="https://futuretools.io/news">FutureTools</a> (Wed): <strong>Runway Ads</strong> connects ad accounts and a brand kit, then agentically generates video and image variants from winners, runs brand checks, publishes approved creatives to Meta, Google, and TikTok, reads performance back, and iterates. Human approval is on by default; teams can later automate by campaign or variant type. Budget guardrails cover max daily spend shift, net-new audience floors, and retargeting caps. Runway says its own Meta/TikTok program since July went from about 77 to about 900 ads per week, 2× ROAS, −41% cost per subscriber, and +34% conversion, with $100M+ ARR attributed to the program. Early access is enterprise pilot only. This is the “agent that spends money” product with the approval switch still default-on.</p>

<h2 id="learn">Something to learn</h2>
<p>A voluntary audit and a civil investigative demand are not the same animal. Tuesday’s White House pact lets labs pick their own outside auditors, publish nothing, and take as long as they want. Trump called it morally binding. Wednesday morning the FTC said it will demand documents and compel executive testimony over the same agent mishaps. When you hear “independent review,” ask who picks the reviewer, whether the findings are public, and whether anyone can force an answer if the company stalls. Handshake and subpoena can land in the same week and still mean opposite things.</p>
<p>A chip company buys a world model lab when the next workload is physical, not just chat. AMD is not paying $8.2B for another inference SDK. World Labs builds spatial models that generate and simulate 3D environments and feed robotics. Lisa Su’s bet is that hardware roadmaps need in-house model research so the silicon matches how those workloads actually move data and memory. When a GPU vendor acquires a frontier lab, ask which workload class they think will set the next rack design, and whether the talent is the product or the models are.</p>
