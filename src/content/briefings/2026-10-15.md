<h2 id="spark">Open weights on a desk</h2>
<p>Meta released <a href="https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open">Muse Spark Open</a> today and called it the most capable US open-weight model to date. Specs: <strong>816B</strong> total parameters, <strong>42B</strong> active (mixture of experts), native multimodal inputs, and a <strong>1M</strong>-token context. The pitch is coding and long-horizon agent work. Weights are on Hugging Face, SageMaker, Google Model Garden, and Microsoft Foundry. Hosted inference is live at Together, Fireworks, Baseten, Modal, Groq, Nebius, and OpenRouter. Engines listed include SGLang, vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, MLX, llama.cpp, and Unsloth.</p>
<p>The desktop claim is the headline next to Wonder. Two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios, each with up to <strong>512 GB</strong> unified memory and about <strong>1.2 TB/s</strong> bandwidth, hold the model under MLX. Meta says it shipped real iOS changes to Ice Cubes (bug fixes, features, full build) and drove DaVinci Resolve end to end on-device with Muse Code. License is the Muse Model License with limited commercial terms. Safety post-training and a model card ship with the release; Meta says it cleared its Superintelligence Scaling Framework thresholds. <a href="https://x.com/AIatMeta/status/2110779754274292212">@AIatMeta</a> posted the launch (about 2,700 likes by afternoon ET). Alexandr Wang amplified the Hugging Face drop. Early chatter puts Spark Open around <strong>39</strong> on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index, ahead of other US open weights but still behind China's GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3. Treat that board number as early until AA publishes a full row.</p>

<h2 id="nemotron">NVIDIA's open agent stack</h2>
<p>Same afternoon NVIDIA opened <a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nemotron-3-5-super-vl/">Nemotron 3.5 Super VL</a>: a <strong>120B</strong>-parameter open multimodal model aimed at always-on agents that read PDFs, charts, tables, web pages, and images in one pass. NVIDIA says it trained on about <strong>26 trillion</strong> tokens, then post-trained with roughly <strong>3.8 million</strong> samples and RL across dozens of environments. Open weights, datasets, and recipes ship with it. Partners named for post-training include Scale (Skydance finance, EY diligence), AfterQuery/Hebbia, Mercor (coding), Ramp, CrowdStrike, Atlassian, Perplexity, and Salesforce. <a href="https://venturebeat.com/technology/nvidias-new-open-nemotron-3-5-super-vl-with-post-training-beats-claude-opus-5-gpt-6-1-sol-on-specialized-task-benchmarks">VentureBeat</a> notes the partner charts show large gains after specialization (medical claims roughly 28% to 72% in one NVIDIA slide), and that those are customized variants on narrow tasks, not a claim that the base model beats Opus or GPT-6.1 Sol generally. Speed claim: up to <strong>5x</strong> faster than a peer open model at a similar Intelligence Index band, measured at about <strong>625</strong> output tokens/sec on four Blackwell B200s. The composite AA index score NVIDIA cites (~26) sits well below flash open models on pure text; vision and document work are the product pitch.</p>
<p>Alongside Super VL: <strong>Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B-A9B 24GB</strong>, a native 2-bit local agent model for RTX/DGX Spark, GeForce, and workstations, live now on Hugging Face with llama.cpp, Ollama, LM Studio, Unsloth, and vLLM. <strong>Bolt E4B 4GB</strong> for conversational and embedded agents lands October 23, timed to RTX Spark laptop launch on the 16th. License is OpenMDW. NIM packaging and a $4,500/GPU/year AI Enterprise quote are the production path; OpenRouter and partner APIs handle hosted trials.</p>

<h2 id="voice">Voice that answers the call</h2>
<p>Wednesday <a href="https://x.ai/news/grok-voice-3">SpaceXSI</a> (the xAI unit) launched <strong>Grok Voice 3.0</strong>, a native speech-to-speech model for the Voice Agent API. Artificial Analysis ranks it <strong>#1</strong> on the Speech-to-Speech Index at <strong>86.9</strong>, ahead of GPT-Live-1 Astra medium (83.2) and Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking (82.6). Component scores from AA (Oct 13): Big Bench Audio reasoning <strong>98.5%</strong>, Speech Agent Arena Elo <strong>1070</strong>, task success <strong>94.8%</strong>, Full Duplex Bench <strong>97%</strong>. On τ-Voice (messy support calls) it is <strong>71.6%</strong>, a hair behind Astra's 74.5%, with about <strong>40%</strong> lower latency to first audio (~0.81s). Price stays <span class="mono">$0.08</span>/min (<span class="mono">$4.80</span>/hr); <span class="mono">grok-voice-latest</span> already points at 3.0. SpaceXSI says it powers millions of Tesla in-car conversations a day and about <strong>20,000</strong> Starlink support calls daily, with roughly <strong>70%</strong> resolved without a human. <a href="https://x.com/SpaceXSI/status/2110416580899168305">@SpaceXSI</a> announced it (about 10,200 likes); <a href="https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2110483960312799675">Elon Musk</a> amplified. Yesterday's Physical briefing missed this because the post landed late Wednesday; it belongs here.</p>

<h2 id="learn">Something to learn</h2>
<p>Why can an 816-billion-parameter model fit on two Mac Studios? Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models do not run all weights on every token. A router picks a small set of "experts" (here about <strong>42B</strong> active out of <strong>816B</strong> total). Memory still has to hold the full weight file, or enough of it to swap without thrashing, but compute per token tracks the active slice. Apple's unified memory puts CPU and GPU on one pool, so two M5 Ultra Studios can present roughly a terabyte of RAM to MLX without PCIe copies. Bandwidth (~1.2 TB/s per machine) matters as much as capacity: each token only needs a thin slice of weights, but that slice must stream fast. When someone says "runs on a desk," ask three numbers: total parameters, active parameters, and memory bandwidth. A dense 816B would not be a desktop story. An MoE with a 42B active path can be, if the memory fabric keeps up.</p>
<p>Speech-to-speech is not "speech recognition plus a text model plus text-to-speech." Cascaded voice stacks turn audio into text, think in text, then speak. Native speech-to-speech models keep audio in the loop: they hear overlapping talk, pause, and interrupt without waiting for a transcript boundary. That is what Full Duplex Bench and τ-Voice measure. Latency to first audio (Grok Voice 3.0 at ~0.81s in AA's run) is the number call centers feel; Arena Elo is whether humans prefer the voice in blind A/Bs; task success is whether the agent actually closed the ticket. When a lab says "best voice model," ask which of those four they won, and whether the stack is native or cascaded. Winning the index while trailing on τ-Voice (as Grok does vs Astra) is a real trade: snappier and cheaper per task, slightly less robust on noisy accents until the next revision.</p>
