<h2 id="suit">Collective restraint</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.local10.com/tech/2026/09/19/lawsuit-says-anthropic-openai-spacexai-and-google-made-illegal-agreement-on-ai-slowdown/">Associated Press</a> (Sat) and <a href="https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/anthropic-openai-spacexai-and-google-face-antitrust-lawsuit-for-agreeing-to-slow-ai-development-plaintiffs-say-plan-has-been-in-motion-for-months-before-calls-agreement-self-serving">Tom’s Hardware</a> (Sun): Four named plaintiffs who pay for ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, or Gemini sued Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI, and Google in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California (complaint filed Friday, case 3:26-cv-10693). The claim is antitrust, not “AI is too dangerous.” The labs may slow themselves. What they may not do, the suit says, is take the “shortcut” of agreeing to “substitute collective restraint for individual accountability,” which would cut the value of paid subscriptions.</p>
<p>The complaint pins the public moment to September 12, when Amodei’s pacing essay landed and Sam Altman, Elon Musk, and Demis Hassabis answered in agreement. It also points earlier, to a July 2026 statement from high-ranking lab employees that admitted “intense competitive pressure not to unilaterally slow” and asked government to back a global slowdown of automated AI development. Amodei’s essay itself flagged antitrust risk and asked for a narrow waiver for safety talks. Altman’s public line welcomed a federal framework but said OpenAI does not need to wait for an exemption to start that work. Sen. Josh Hawley has already said there is “no world” in which he gives the biggest tech firms an antitrust holiday. Lab spokespeople did not immediately comment to AP on Saturday.</p>
<p>Safety as a shared project. Competition law as the fence. The suit is early, thin on private emails, and heavy on public statements. The useful read is the split it forces: you can believe pacing is wise and still ask whether rivals may lock arms without Congress or a court saying they can.</p>

<h2 id="force">AI Force</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-says-he-will-create-ai-force-name-ai-czar-2026-09-19/">Reuters</a> (Sat) and <a href="https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/997867/trump-ai-force-ai-czar">The Verge</a> (Sun): Trump posted on Truth Social that he will appoint a new “AI czar” and create an “AI Force,” comparing it to Space Force, and that only “High I.Q. individuals need apply.” No structure, budget, timeline, or name. The same post said the administration will not hinder AI growth, will watch for “BAD” with existing criminal and civil law, and claimed without evidence that data centers raise salaries, cut taxes, and make streets safer. David Sacks held the czar role earlier and left for an external advisory seat; he has argued product liability beats new federal rules. The post lands days before a scheduled Trump–Xi meeting and on a weekend when Treasury’s Scott Bessent was set to meet China’s He Lifeng in New York on AI security and trade.</p>
<p>Industry asks for a waiver to talk about slowing down. The White House answers with a force and a czar and a rejection of new guardrails. Same week, different movie.</p>

<h2 id="also">Also today</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.buchodi.com/chatgpt-now-knows-what-you-do-on-other-websites-via-ad-collector/">buchodi</a> (Sun): A detailed capture of OpenAI’s ad collector at <span class="mono">bzr.openai.com</span>. ChatGPT mints a short-lived JWT that binds your account to an <span class="mono">obi</span> identifier, then sets an <span class="mono">__obi</span> cookie on <span class="mono">.openai.com</span> with <span class="mono">SameSite=None</span> for a year. When advertiser sites load OpenAI’s pixel SDK, that cookie rides along with page and conversion events. The researcher reproduced it on Chrome for Android across Chewy, Wayfair, Eventbrite, and others, and notes Safari’s ITP blocks the third-party cookie. OpenAI’s cookie policy lists <span class="mono">__obi</span> under analytics. Press and privacy mail got a support ack without answers to the classification questions. Meta built this join years ago. Running it on a chat product that hears private prompts is the new part.</p>

<h2 id="learn">Something to learn</h2>
<p>Antitrust cares who decides to slow down. One company can ship later for safety and call that product judgment. Several rivals announcing the same slowdown together can look like an agreement to sell less capability than competition would produce, which is the kind of coordination U.S. antitrust law has always hated, even when the motive is noble. That is why Amodei asked for a narrow government waiver for safety talks, why Altman talked about a federal framework, and why Hawley said he will not hand Big Tech an exemption. The subscriber suit is not arguing “safety is fake.” It is arguing “you do not get to write the rules for each other in a private club.” When a lab says “industry pacing,” ask whether it is a unilateral calendar, a statute, or a handshake among competitors.</p>
<p>A third-party cookie with <span class="mono">SameSite=None</span> is the plumbing that lets one company join your chat account to what you do on other people’s sites. OpenAI’s <span class="mono">__obi</span> is minted while you are on ChatGPT, stored on <span class="mono">.openai.com</span>, then sent automatically when an advertiser page loads OpenAI’s pixel. Analytics consent can be enough to turn it on. Safari blocks it. Chrome on Android does not. The join is old adtech. The weird part is wiring it to a product people treat like a private notebook. When a chat app says it has ads, ask which cookie crosses sites, which consent bucket covers it, and whether “analytics” is doing marketing’s job.</p>
