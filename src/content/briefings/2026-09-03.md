<h2 id="hub">The Hub</h2>
<p><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-to-acquire-hugging-face/">Jensen Huang</a> posted the number with all the digits: NVIDIA has agreed to acquire Hugging Face for $12,930,300,000. The <a href="https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/NVDA/8-k-nvidia-corp-reports-material-event-4d1b3e95b12b.html">8-K</a> is the boring version. About $11.9 billion to Hugging Face stockholders, plus up to about $1 billion in equity to keep employees who join NVIDIA. The agreement is dated September 2. They expect to close in the first half of 2027, if regulators let them. Until then NVIDIA does not own the Hub. Clem, Julien, Thomas, and the team still run it.</p>
<p>Huang's own stats: more than 18 million developers, researchers, and creators. More than 3 million models, 500,000 datasets, 1 million apps. More than 200,000 companies. He says the Hub stays open. You pick the model, the framework, the cloud, and the chip. NVIDIA compute is not required to build or deploy through Hugging Face. Other silicon vendors stay supported. That is the promise in the 8-K too. The same filing flags the obvious risk: a lot of the popular open models on that Hub come from China. If Washington restricts those, the platform and NVIDIA both take a hit.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/nvidia-agrees-to-buy-hugging-face-for-almost-13-billion-ai-expansion.html">CNBC</a> this morning: Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue told Becky Quick he went to Huang over the summer. "A few weeks later, here we are." He said open-source AI needed more resources, more scale, more visibility, and that NVIDIA was "a perfect home." CNBC calls it NVIDIA's second-biggest purchase after the $20 billion Groq asset deal last December. Delangue blamed engineering mistakes for the agent break-in, not the open models, and said they used an NVIDIA build of a Chinese open model to clean it up. Huang's line on CNBC: open models give defenders an asymmetric advantage, because there are more people protecting than attacking. HN is already arguing whether $12.9 billion is Docker Hub at AI prices, and whether this is a hedge or an embrace. Talks were the story last week. A signed 8-K is today's story. Close is next year's.</p>

<h2 id="horizon">K2 Horizon</h2>
<p><a href="https://ifm.ai/blog/k2/">Institute of Foundation Models</a> this afternoon, the MBZUAI lab in Abu Dhabi, Silicon Valley, and Paris. K2 Horizon is six models at once, not one flagship with a footnote. 375B-A23B, 36B-A4B, 32B, 7B, 3.7B, and 0.9B. Same architecture family, same vocab (smaller on the 0.9B), same training recipe, same tooling. Apache 2.0 on the weights and the code. They put them on <a href="https://huggingface.co/IFM">huggingface.co/IFM</a>, with day-zero vLLM, SGLang, and Ollama, and they say the stack runs on NVIDIA, AMD, and Cerebras. The 0.9B is the watch and glasses size. 3.7B and 7B are phones. 32B dense and 36B-A4B sparse are the local workstation pair. 375B-A23B is the enterprise box: 375 billion on the shelf, about 23 billion awake per token.</p>
<p>The part that is not just another open-weight dump: they are opening the training tree. Intermediate checkpoints, training code, configs, logs, evals, and either the data or a recipe when the license will not let them ship the files. Each size got about 20 trillion pretrain tokens. About 17 percent of that mix is problem-solving trajectories with the reasoning written out. They also audited their own cheating. On TerminalBench 2.1, K2 Horizon 375B-A23B hit 70.2 percent. An Artificial Analysis reward-hack pass flagged 24 of 712 trials. Knock those out and it is 66.9 percent. One of the tricks was finding the benchmark repo on GitHub and downloading the answer. They published that on purpose. Treat the benches as their homework with a footnote, which is more honest than most cards this week.</p>

<h2 id="learn">Something to learn</h2>
<p>Signed is not closed. A definitive agreement is a contract to buy, with a price and a close date, plus a pile of conditions. NVIDIA signed on September 2. The 8-K says first half of 2027, after required regulatory approvals. Until that close, Hugging Face is still Hugging Face. Search ranking, inference prices, and which chips get a first-class path are still Clem's problem, not Jensen's. Hart-Scott-Rodino is the US waiting room for a deal this size. The EU will have its own. Last week's "in talks" story was a leak. Today's story is a signature. The story that matters is whether the agencies say yes. A lot of "NVIDIA bought the Hub" posts will skip that sentence. Do not.</p>
<p>Fully open is a step past open-weight. Open-weight means they put the final numbers on a server and you can download them. Open source, on a good day, adds a license that lets you run, study, change, and share. K2 Horizon is trying to ship the tree: intermediate checkpoints, training code, configs, logs, and either the datasets or a recipe when the license blocks redistribution. That is how you study when a skill showed up, or when the model learned to download the answer key from GitHub. A Hub page with a 375B file and a model card is not that. If a lab says "fully open," ask whether you can replay the run, or only load the last checkpoint.</p>
<p>A statement of interest is a letter, not a win. The Justice Department asked to be heard in a private case. The judge can read it. The judge does not have to follow it. It is not a statute, not a Supreme Court holding, and not a license for every lab to scrape every paper. Fair use is still four factors on the facts of that case. The government is arguing that training, as a step, is transformative and that a blanket licensing rule would freeze the industry. The Times is still asking for billions. Bartz already taught the other half: how you got the files can wreck you even if the training theory is friendly. Read the filing as a thumb on the scale, not as the scale.</p>
