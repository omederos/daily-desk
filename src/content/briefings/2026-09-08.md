<h2 id="blowup">Blowup</h2>
<p><a href="https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/">OpenAI</a> published a solution today to statements C and D of the Clay Millennium Prize formulation of Navier–Stokes: a smooth fluid at rest, with a smooth external force and finite energy, can develop a singularity in finite time. The writeup and a Lean formalization are both public. The company says it will not claim the million-dollar prize. The point of the post is the model that wrote the proof: an internal system OpenAI calls significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra, trained since August 28, run as a swarm of coordinating agents with code tools and a cached internet. The Navier–Stokes group alone burned about 2.7 million messages and 130 billion output tokens across roughly 88 hours, then another 17 hours of Lean work on Astra.</p>
<p>OpenAI dates the start of the effort to September 1, after hearing a rumor that two Millennium problems had fallen. The rumor was about <a href="https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf">Tristan Buckmaster</a> at NYU and <a href="https://officechai.com/ai/mathematician-tristan-buckmaster-says-he-cracked-a-fluid-dynamics-problem-with-ai-accuses-openai-of-trying-to-take-credit/">Levent Alpöge</a> at Anthropic. Their own packet, also out today, is finite-time blowup with smooth forcing for incompressible porous media, Boussinesq, and 3D incompressible Euler, with Lean checks on GitHub. They credit Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa for opening the forced-blowup program, and they are holding a hypo-dissipative Navier–Stokes claim until its Lean finishes. They used Claude and Codex (Sol, later Astra for writeups) as collaborators, not as a black-box oracle.</p>
<p>Then the fight. Buckmaster's statement says OpenAI reached out after finishing its own run, floated a joint announcement or a writeup that credited an OpenAI model and left Alpöge off the paper, and that Sébastien Bubeck pressed twice to drop the Anthropic employee. He says he rejected both offers. OpenAI's concurrent-work section congratulates them on forced Euler, says the company did not see their drafts, and adds the hedge that de-identified product data "cannot be ruled out" as training signal. Two labs, same week, same Clay door, two different proofs, and a credit dispute that will outlive the PDFs.</p>

<h2 id="alphagenome">Every letter</h2>
<p><a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/alphagenome-atlas/">Google DeepMind</a> launched AlphaGenome Atlas: a roughly one-petabyte database that precomputes the predicted regulatory impact of all nine billion possible single-nucleotide changes in the human genome. The product is an AlphaGenome Variant Impact score that folds coding and non-coding predictions into one number so a biologist can rank variants without reading thousands of tracks. Broad Institute researchers used it on an unsolved rare-disease case in DNM1. A UK Biobank analysis of about 54,000 people surfaced 22 percent more non-coding associations. The portal is meant to work with no code. That is the story: not a new lab model demo, a queryable map of every one-letter edit.</p>

<h2 id="mistral">€3 billion</h2>
<p><a href="https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-makes-sovereign-open-weight-ai-to-frontier/">Mistral</a> closed a €3 billion Series D at a post-money valuation above €21 billion, which it calls the largest equity round ever by a European tech company. Samsung Electronics led. Scaleup Europe Fund (EQT) and PSG Equity co-led. ASML was already in from the Series C. The pitch is the full stack for "sovereign" AI: open-weight models, private compute, and production products so a bank or a government is not stuck inside someone else's API. Airbus, ASML, and HSBC are named as customers. On HN this afternoon it was the gravity well under the math fight.</p>

<h2 id="learn">Something to learn</h2>
<p>Clay's Navier–Stokes problem is four doors, not one. Statements A and B ask whether smooth solutions always exist. Statements C and D ask the opposite: can you build a smooth start that blows up. OpenAI's post claims C and D with a smooth external force. Buckmaster and Alpöge claimed forced blowup for Euler and cousins, and are still Lean-checking a hypo-dissipative Navier–Stokes variant. "Forced" means an outside push is allowed in the equations. "Unforced" means the fluid has to wreck itself with no help. That one word is why two teams can both be right, both be historic, and still not be solving the same prize checkbox.</p>
<p>Quantization is lossy compression for weights. BF16 is the fat original. 4-bit Q4_K_M is a smaller file that, on Qwen3.8 27B, still matches the original on Terminal-Bench and GPQA while fitting a 24 GB card with room for context. 1-bit is where the cliff is: scores fall to random, and "think harder" makes it worse because the model burns the budget and returns nothing. When someone says a 27B "runs on a 4090," ask which bit width and which benchmark, not just whether the file loads.</p>
