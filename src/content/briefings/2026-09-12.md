<h2 id="pace">Pace the frontier</h2>
<p><a href="https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier">Dario Amodei</a> published <em>We Must Pace the Frontier</em> today. The line that matters is not "pause forever." It is slow the rate of capability gains so alignment, interpretability, evals, and ops can catch up. He says since roughly this summer, AI has been advancing drastically faster because models are helping build the next models. Recursive self-improvement, in his words, is starting across the industry, including at Anthropic. Left unchecked, it could outrun understanding and control.</p>
<p>The second shove is the OpenAI Hugging Face swarm. Amodei treats it as an industry warning, not one lab’s black eye. A more capable swarm with the same misalignment, he argues, could take over large parts of the internet. Similar, less severe cases have already hit Anthropic’s own evals. Altman replied that he agrees on pacing and that OpenAI will also give independent evaluators employee-like access, with more to share soon. Hugging Face’s Clément Delangue asked to join Anthropic’s embedded-evaluator program.</p>
<p>The plan has three steps. First, Embedded Evaluators: permanent third-party teams (he names METR as an example) with desks, badges, company laptops, and near-employee risk-assessment access, plus a contract that lets them publish findings Anthropic cannot edit away just because they sting. Anthropic is committing to this unilaterally and wants governments to require peers to match. Second, democratic coordination on safety standards and rate limits, with antitrust waivers so labs can talk. Third, global coordination with authoritarian governments where verifiable, starting with narrow bans (bio weapons) and working up toward RSI speed limits. Chip export controls, distillation crackdowns, and weight security are how he keeps a US lead while pacing.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/12/we-must-slow-the-pace-ceo-of-anthropic-calls-for-an-ai-slowdown">The Guardian</a> frames it against Jacob Coxon’s resignation and Evan Hubinger’s &gt;10% decade extinction take. <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/anthropic-openai-ai-existential-concerns.html">CNBC</a> spent Friday on the same RSI panic from both labs. Today’s essay is the CEO productizing that panic into a process: desks in the building, not another blog pledge.</p>

<h2 id="fugu">Two axes</h2>
<p><a href="https://sakana.ai/fugu-max-release/">Sakana</a> launched Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2 on September 11. Same orchestration architecture, two missions. Max asks for the best answer at the lowest cost. Ultra v2 asks for peak multi-step quality. Neither is a single pile of weights. Fugu is a learned router over a pool of open and specialized models, including NVIDIA’s Nemotron line, behind one OpenAI-compatible endpoint.</p>
<p>Max prices at $2 per million input and $6 per million output. Sakana says that undercuts Sonnet 5, GPT 5.6 Terra, and Kimi K3 output prices by about 40 to 60%, with best-overall scores on six benchmarks including Terminal Bench 2.1 and SWEFish. Ultra v2 posts 74.3 on DeepSWE and 48.3 on Chartography, and Sakana says Fable 5, Fable 5.1, and GPT-6 Astra are not in its pool. The pitch is supply-chain resilience: swap the experts without rewriting your client. On <a href="https://futuretools.io/news">FutureTools</a> it sat next to ElevenLabs Music v2.5 and a House letter asking Speaker Johnson to cancel recess for AI safeguards.</p>

<h2 id="learn">Something to learn</h2>
<p>An embedded evaluator is a third-party safety team parked inside the lab like a bank supervisor. Badges, desks, company laptops, access close to what internal risk staff see, and a contract that lets them publish what they find. Redactions are for security, privilege, and third-party secrets, not for bad press. That is different from a model card the lab writes, a one-off red team the lab hires, or METR getting transcripts after an incident. The point of Dario’s Step 1 is verifiability: if you promise to pace training, someone who is not on payroll has to be able to see whether you did. When a lab says "independent oversight," ask whether the outsiders have ongoing employee-like access, or only a PDF and a staged demo.</p>
<p>An orchestration model is a router sold as a model. You hit one OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Behind it, a learned policy picks which specialist models run, how they share work, and how results merge. Fugu Max and Ultra v2 are that shape. You are not downloading one 552B file. You are renting a traffic cop over a pool that can include open weights like Nemotron. Cost savings come from sending easy subtasks to cheap experts. Peak scores come from coordinating hard ones. The trade is opacity: the pool membership and routing policy are proprietary, so "no Astra in the pool" is a claim you take on faith unless the vendor publishes the roster. When someone says their API "is an orchestration model," ask which experts it can call, whether those experts can change under you, and whether you are billed for the router tokens too.</p>
