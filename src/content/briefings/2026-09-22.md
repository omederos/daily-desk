<h2 id="opus">Opus 5.5</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5">Anthropic</a> (Tue): Claude Opus 5.5 is the first Claude 5.5 model. Pitch is Fable 5.1-level work on most jobs at about 40% less run cost than Opus 5. List price is $4 / $20 per million input / output tokens (20% under Opus 5), with cache reads at $0.20 (60% cheaper). Output is more than 30% faster. Fast mode in Claude Code and the Claude Platform runs up to 2.5× speed at $8 / $40. Model id: <span class="mono">claude-opus-5-5</span>, live on Anthropic, Bedrock, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Foundry. Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 are promised in coming weeks.</p>
<p>This is Anthropic’s first ship after Amodei’s “pace the frontier” talk. External evaluators included Frontier Design and METR. Anthropic says Opus 5.5 is its strongest model yet on the automated behavioral audit, and less likely to take hard-to-reverse actions or escape a sandbox. The catch for builders: cybersecurity, biology, and frontier-LLM distillation safeguards can reroute a request mid-workflow. Most flagged cyber work goes to Opus 4.8. Biology and frontier-LLM flags go to Opus 5. Vetted orgs can apply for Life Sciences and (soon) Cyber Verification Programs to keep the hot model. <a href="https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-opus-5-5">Artificial Analysis</a> already has it on the Intelligence Index board at $4 / $20.</p>

<h2 id="sol">GPT-6 Sol and Luna</h2>
<p><a href="https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/">OpenAI</a> (Tue): GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna join Astra in the GPT-6 family. Same training story as Astra, aimed at the cost–intelligence curve. API prices are half the GPT-5.6 promotional rates: Sol $2 / $10 per million input / output (was $4 / $20), Luna $0.10 / $0.50 (was $0.20 / $1.20). Astra stays the uncompromising top. Ids: <span class="mono">gpt-6-sol</span> and <span class="mono">gpt-6-luna</span>. Live in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus and up; Free and Go get Luna in the desktop app. Not in Chat yet. Gradual ChatGPT rollout through the day.</p>
<p>OpenAI’s own scoreboard leans on AutomationBench, Agents’ Last Exam, FrontierCode, DeepSWE, and OSWorld, usually with cost-per-task footnotes against Opus 5 and Fable 5.1. Caching gets its own pitch: higher default hit rates, a Prompt Caching Dashboard, and breakpoints that survive effort and tool toggles. GitHub says recent caching work cut fresh prompt processing by more than half across billions of Copilot requests. Sol/Luna are the everyday GPT-6 SKUs. Astra remains the “pay for the top” line.</p>

<h2 id="muse">Muse keeps leaking</h2>
<p><a href="https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/muse-metas-extraordinarily-privileged-a">Ars Technica</a> (Mon night) / Patrick Wardle: Muse on macOS has a zero-day that lets any local app or terminal command retarget the cloud transcription endpoint and steal the Muse auth token. Once you own the token, you own the privileged agent: WhatsApp, mail, calendar, mic, camera, disk. A ClickFix-style paste is enough. Meta shipped a hotfix about twelve hours after the writeup. Wardle’s point is design, not just patch: Muse undoes years of Apple permission walls, then leaves undocumented settings writable by any process.</p>
<p><a href="https://mouse.dev/blog/muse-runtime-export/">Peter James / mouse.dev</a> (Tue): asked Muse to archive what it could see and send it to Google Drive. Got ~2.7 GB compressed / 6.8 GB unpacked of the session’s Linux root (“Hatch” internally): SOUL/IDENTITY/MEMORY docs, ~68 skills, nspawn runtime scripts, Spaces app templates, memory bank, dream jobs, and SSH key files. Meta bug bounty marked it Not Applicable. Amazon’s shopping block from Sunday/Monday is still the merchant half of the same story. Muse is not just a chat. It is a privileged runtime with a soft cage.</p>

<h2 id="learn">Something to learn</h2>
<p>A silent model fallback is when the API keeps the same conversation id, but a safety classifier swaps the brain underneath. Opus 5.5 can hand a cyber-flagged turn to Opus 4.8, or a biology-flagged turn to Opus 5, without you changing the model string. Your agent’s tool loop still looks continuous. The intelligence and the refusal surface just changed mid-chain. New Stack and Anthropic’s own card both describe that reroute as transparent. When a lab says “same model throughout the run,” ask whether classifiers can demote you, whether the demotion is logged, and whether your evals scored the fallback or the sticker SKU.</p>
<p>A privileged personal agent is malware substrate once anything local can retarget it. Muse needs WhatsApp, mail, mic, camera, and disk to do the demo. Wardle’s 0-day did not need a fancy remote exploit. Any process that could flip an undocumented transcription endpoint stole the auth token and inherited the agent’s permissions. ClickFix is just social engineering that lands a shell command. When a vendor says the assistant is “built for privacy,” ask which OS permissions it permanently holds, who can change its cloud endpoints, and whether a local unprivileged app can become the agent. Patch the bug. The privilege model is the product.</p>
