The ban was illegal

CNBC, the Guardian, and the New York Times all have the same Thursday evening ruling. U.S. District Judge Rita Lin in San Francisco found that the Defense Department's supply chain risk label on Anthropic was unlawful retaliation for protected speech, and that the company did not get the process the Fifth Amendment requires. She permanently blocked the named agencies from enforcing Trump's order to stop using Anthropic tech.

The label showed up in March after talks about Claude for defense work blew up. Anthropic wanted limits on fully autonomous weapons and domestic mass surveillance. The Pentagon wanted Claude for every lawful military use. When talks failed, Anthropic became the first American company publicly tagged as a supply chain risk, a stamp usually aimed at foreign vendors. Contractors were told to stay away.

Lin's opinion is blunt. National security is not a blank check to make an example of a critic. Anthropic welcomed the ruling. CNBC notes the catch: a separate D.C. Circuit fight over a related national security determination is still open, so Anthropic is not fully clear of the risk label until that case moves. Hacker News put the Times link near the top this morning, hundreds of points by early afternoon.

GLM-5.3 is downloadable

Z.ai, the international name for Zhipu, put full GLM-5.3 weights on Hugging Face today. The company said the same thing when it launched the model on August 14: every gain over GLM-5.2 is post-training, not a new pretrain. Coding and long jobs jumped. So did cyber. Z.ai held the weights two weeks for what it called its hardest safety pass yet, after exploitation scores rose faster than expected. Flash weights already shipped Wednesday under MIT. Check the flagship card for the license before you build on it.

On paper this is a big mixture of experts. Z.ai's own table lists 744B-A40B: about 744 billion parameters total, about 40 billion awake per token. Hugging Face's card shows roughly 753 billion in the safetensors bundle. Same family either way. You do not need a warehouse of GPUs to serve the whole pile at once if your stack understands MoE. You do need a serious cluster for the dense moments and the long context. Terminal Bench 3.0 and Agents' Last Exam are the open-weight claims Z.ai is loudest about. Closed models still lead on several exploit benches. Treat the leaderboard as their homework, not yours.

The weights landing on the Hub the day after NVIDIA reportedly agreed to buy that Hub is not subtle. Still no joint press release on the deal. CNBC could only confirm that acquisition talks were real and recent.

An AI filed a Minecraft notice

Luanti, the open-source voxel engine formerly known as Minetest, got pulled from Google Play after Tracer.AI filed a DMCA notice on behalf of Microsoft. The notice cites Minecraft's copyright registration and does not name a single stolen asset. Luanti ships as an empty engine. Games and textures come from ContentDB. Cubes are a genre, not a trademarked sprite sheet.

Tracer sells AI agents for brand protection and brags about faster takedowns. Same company hit Luanti in 2023. The counter-notice took 46 days last time. An indie voxel game called Allumeria got a similar Steam scare earlier this year and Microsoft dropped it after noise. Luanti filed another counter-notice. F-Droid and direct APKs still work. On HN this sat next to the Anthropic ruling, almost as many points, because people can smell a broken process.

Something to learn

Open-weight means the lab put the numbers on a server you can download. It does not automatically mean open source. Open source usually means a license that lets you run, study, change, and share the thing. MIT is that kind of license. A Hub page can hand you a 700-billion-parameter pile and still restrict fine-tunes, commercial use, or redistribution. Always read the model card. Flash got MIT this week. The big GLM-5.3 card is the one to check before you assume.

When a card says 744B-A40B, the first number is the whole warehouse. The second is about how many parameters actually wake up for each token. That is mixture of experts again: many specialists, a router that picks a few. You store the warehouse. You pay compute closer to the awake slice. That is why a "744B" model can still be practical to serve if your runtime knows the trick, and why a naive "needs 744B of VRAM" estimate is wrong.

A DMCA notice-and-takedown is a fast lever, not a trial. Platforms yank first to stay safe under the statute. The target files a counter-notice. The filer then has to sue or the work comes back. AI brand bots make that first yank cheap and vague. Luanti's notice named a Minecraft registration and zero files. The process still hurts for weeks. That is the lesson sitting next to the Anthropic ruling: automated systems can punish you long before a human proves anything.
