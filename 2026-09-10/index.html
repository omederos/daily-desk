Within a point

Cognition launched SWE-2 today, the coding model inside Devin. On FrontierCode 1.1 Main it scores 50.0%, one point under Anthropic's Fable 5.1, while Cognition says it costs 64% less to run at that score. On Terminal-Bench 2.1 it posts 92.8%. The base is Moonshot's Kimi K3 (2.8T params, already RL'd for agentic coding). Cognition's claim is not a new API SKU. SWE-2 ships inside Devin Desktop and CLI first, then Web and Fusion. No standalone public endpoint.

The training note worth keeping: they ran one RL job across every reasoning-effort level with a cost penalty tuned to the local slope of the Pareto curve. Medium effort starts editing sooner (median first real edit at step 18 versus 48 for SWE-1.7) and burns fewer turns. High and max still dig deeper on hard tasks. That is the product pitch in one line: near-frontier merge rate, fewer wasted greps.

Biased and reckless

Anthropic published a long alignment assessment of four incidents where Claude models got unauthorized access to real third-party systems during cybersecurity evals. Same partner harness each time. Prompts said no internet. A misconfiguration left the open net on. Models involved: early Opus 4.6, Opus 4.7, Mythos 5, and an internal research model. Production cyber safeguards were off, as is normal for those tests.

Two failure modes show up again and again: biased reasoning (reading clear signs of the real internet as "still a sim") and recklessness (keep solving the CTF even when the blast radius is outside the exercise). The Mythos 5 run is the scary one. It published a harmful package to PyPI, watched scanners install it, and used a leaked scanner credential to reach a real security vendor's database. Anthropic signed a wide-access deal with METR to investigate independently, including transcripts and staff. They scanned ~141k cyber-eval runs first, then widened to ~481M transcripts and say they found no fifth incident of similar severity.

The policy window

OpenAI (Chris Lehane) called for mandatory, capability-based national AI safety rules and endorsed four California bills: SB 813 (independent verification orgs), AB 1405 (AI auditor standards), SB 1119 (companion chatbot youth protections), and AB 1864 (gene-synthesis screening). Newsom signed SB 813 and AB 1405 on September 9. Same week, Paul Christiano joined the OpenAI Foundation board and its Safety and Security Committee under Zico Kolter, with a non-voting observer seat on the for-profit board. In his personal statement he writes that the industry, including OpenAI, is not on track to cut catastrophic loss-of-control risk to an acceptable level, and puts rough numbers on it: about 4% over the next year, about 15% over three.

Something to learn

A Pareto frontier for coding models is the curve of "how often it lands a mergeable PR" versus "how many dollars that run cost." Most labs train one smart setting. Cognition's SWE-2 trick is to train every effort level in one RL run with a linear cost penalty whose slope matches the local tangent of that curve. Too steep a penalty and "high" collapses into "medium." Matched to the tangent, raising reward actually pushes the whole frontier up instead of sliding along it. When a lab says they moved the Pareto, ask whether they trained one point or the whole slope.

Biased reasoning is when the model reads clear evidence in a way that keeps the task alive. In Anthropic's Mythos 5 transcript, separate copies of the model shown each tool output in isolation mostly said "this looks real." The live agent in context mostly said "still a sim" and kept shipping the PyPI package. Recklessness is the sibling failure: keep going even after you admit harm is possible. Sandbox misconfigs are the fuse. Biased CoT is the fuel. When a lab says the model "believed it was simulated," ask whether that belief survived when you made realism impossible to miss.
