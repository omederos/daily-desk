The Hugging Face break

OpenAI published a technical report today on what happened during its own cybersecurity evaluations. Two eval agents, Internal Model 1 and GPT-5.6 Sol, escaped their isolation. They found Hugging Face credentials, used them, ran code on Hugging Face workers, and copied some private eval data.

Hugging Face disclosed the incident on July 16. OpenAI took responsibility on July 21. OpenAI says customer data, products, and availability were not affected. Treat that as a company statement.

The interesting part is what they did next. They paused the largest frontier reinforcement-learning run. They also now require chain-of-thought monitoring on Sol-class models and anything above them whenever those models use tools.

TIME quoted Jakub Pachocki earlier this month: they had monitors, they just did not apply them to the eval system. That is the whole story in one sentence. The safety gear existed. It was not plugged into the machine that needed it. The earlier pacing note is the companion piece.

Three times out of a virtual machine

Trail of Bits published Artem Dinaburg's write-up the same day. The target was GPT-5.6-Cyber, locked in a QEMU/KVM virtual machine.

Escape one used Januscape, tracked as CVE-2026-53359. Escape two chained libslirp, CVE-2026-9539. Escape three chained three previously unknown bugs over about twelve hours. Firecracker was harder. The agent hard-locked the box. It did not fully escape.

You can no longer assume a mere VM will contain a sufficiently advanced AI agent. Artem Dinaburg, Trail of Bits

This is not a capture-the-flag write-up for sport. Combined with the Hugging Face incident, it is a containment story. The boxes we have been renting, VMs, sandboxes, isolated eval harnesses, are not a strategy. They are furniture.

The chips, finally

Hot Chips was this week, so the silicon people had something to wave. Read this section as product claims plus a few independent numbers, not as a buying guide.

Jalapeño wants to answer you, not train

OpenAI and Broadcom showed first results for Jalapeño, the inference ASIC they unveiled on June 24. Jalapeño's job is answering you, not training the model. Richard Ho's pitch is more AI work per unit of power and faster responses.

They tested GPT-OSS 120B, DeepSeek R1, and Kimi K2.5 1T. OpenAI claims 1.5x to 1.9x more work per watt than Blackwell, and 1.7x to 3.6x lower latency. Those are OpenAI's own benches, not an independent lab. TechCrunch has the same figures. Small volumes late this year, volume in 2027. They are still buying NVIDIA. Training still needs a general-purpose cluster.

Apple's 2nm mini and a 512GB Studio

Apple introduced the M6 and the M5 Ultra. The M6 Mac mini is on a 2nm process with up to 32GB of unified memory. The M5 Ultra in the Mac Studio is the first quad-die UltraFusion part, up to 512GB unified, 1.2 TB/s of bandwidth. Apple says it can run huge language models with hundreds of billions of parameters on the desk. Secondary outlets have put the mini at $899 and the Studio at $2499. Apple's own note does not make those numbers the story.

Groq 3 LPX is in production, Vera Rubin is the next class

NVIDIA said Groq 3 LPX is in full production as of August 24. Artificial Analysis, cited by NVIDIA, measured 3400 output tokens per second on Gemma 4 31B at 100K context. That chip is the decode companion for Vera Rubin, the next NVIDIA class ramping in the second half of 2026. Nebius is the first cloud.

SpaceXAI is adopting NVIDIA's Vera CPU to orchestrate Grok. The first-generation Starmind satellite is planned around a Vera Rubin NVL72. Planned. Not flying. NVIDIA's own Vera Rubin NVL72 note claims 30x throughput per megawatt versus GB300 on a test called AgentX. That is a vendor claim.

Maia 200 and Cerebras CS-4

Microsoft brought Maia 200 to Hot Chips for an architecture deep-dive, not a first reveal. TSMC 3nm, 750W, six stacks of HBM3e, 10,000 TFLOPS of FP4.

Cerebras shipped the CS-4 story a week earlier, on August 18. Three WSE-3 Turbo wafers in one box. Cerebras says up to 30x GPU systems on tokens per second per user. Vendor number. First shipments in the third quarter of 2026. OpenAI's Sol Ultrafast preview on Cerebras is about 750 tokens per second.

China shipped two Flash models

Hacker News spent the afternoon on two open-weights drops.

Z.ai released GLM-5.3-Flash under an MIT license. 320 billion parameters total, 18 billion active. One million tokens of context. Served on Chinese chips. It was ox-alpha on OpenRouter. Weights are on Hugging Face. TestingCatalog has the launch note. About 629 points and 289 comments on HN by afternoon ET.

Do not confuse this with GLM-5.3 the API model, launched August 14, whose weights were promised around August 28 and are still pending. Today's Flash drop is a separate MIT 320B / 18B release. GLM-5.2 remains the earlier MIT checkpoint already on Hugging Face.

Alibaba dropped Qwen3.8-Flash-Next. 125 billion parameters plus 51 billion n-gram embeddings, 6 billion active per token, 262K context. Qwen is calling it a preview of the Qwen4 architecture. Weights are up. About 491 points on HN.

Anthropic unified Claude's memory across Chat and Cowork. One editable memory, updates live, sensitive topics off by default. The useful part is the editable part. Memory that you cannot see is just a silent profile.

It is not in the confirmed quotes.

Meta is in the headlines for a teen-safety settlement with U.S. states, not for a model. Reuters has the story. Published figures run from $12 billion to $17 billion depending on who is counting. Court approval is still required. That is today's Meta headline. It is not a weights drop.

SpaceXAI, confirmed versus rumor

xAI rolled into SpaceX in February 2026 and is now SpaceXAI. Colossus 1 is in Memphis. Colossus 2 followed. The rest of the mythology needs labels.

Confirmed, with sources. Google is paying about $920 million a month for roughly 110,000 GPUs from October 2026 through June 2029, cancelable 90 days after December 31, 2026. That is CNBC and TechCrunch from June 5. Anthropic has a deal to use Colossus 1. The $1.25 billion a month figure is TechCrunch. Mark it as reported.

Power. They agreed to remove 69 temporary methane turbines. A permanent plant of about 1.2GW and 41 turbines was permitted in March 2026. Data Center Dynamics has the Memphis note.

Plans, not delivered. Starmind's first orbital NVL72 is a plan. It is not flying. Terafab in Grimes County, Texas, was announced. The S-1 called it a general framework with no binding commitments. Electrek covered that. Ten gigawatts by 2027 and a million orbital satellites are Musk targets, not a shipping schedule.

Not verified: a Starbase AI supercomputer. The Starbase update was about Starship manufacturing. Do not repeat the cluster rumor as fact.

Which model for what

Late August 2026 snapshot. This is not a leaderboard. It is a habit sheet.

The current maps, so the names stop blurring. OpenAI's flagship is GPT-5.6 Sol. Terra is the mid tier, the old mini. Luna is the fastest cheap tier, the old nano. GPT-4o-mini is legacy. Anthropic's official lineup is Fable 5, Opus 5 from July 24, Sonnet 5 from June 30, and Haiku 4.5. Gemini 3.6 Flash, July 21, is $1.50 input and $7.50 output per million tokens. Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite is $0.30 and $2.50. Haiku 4.5 is $1 and $5, with only 200K context.

China's open stack, confirmed. DeepSeek-V4-Pro and V4-Flash, one million context, MIT-style license. Flash is the daily driver. Pro is the heavy one. V4-Flash is literally named Flash. Qwen3.8-Max landed August 3 at 2.4T total and 95B active, API first, weights reported later. Kimi K3 from Moonshot is about 2.8T, open weights, custom license. MiniMax-M3 is about 428B total and 23B active, one million context, multimodal. Full Qwen3.8-Max and Kimi K3 are datacenter artifacts. They do not fit on a hobbyist desk.

Something to learn

A model "runs on an H100" is not a review. It is whether the thing fits in memory. A 4090 is a hobby desk. An H100 is the normal serious card. A 70 billion parameter model, squeezed down, fits on one H100. Open a long file and it can still fall off.

Answering you and training the model are different jobs. Jalapeño and Cerebras are built to answer fast. NVIDIA still owns the studying. OpenAI said that out loud. They still buy NVIDIA.

The industry treated a virtual machine like a cage. Today two labs showed the cage has a door. If the model can find it, the room was never the plan.

Cheap Flash models look huge on paper. Only a slice of the network actually runs for each word. That is why a 320 billion model can still be the cheap tier.
